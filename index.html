The problem

Market data forgets. Aggregators average venues into one price, fill gaps with zeros, and overwrite history in place — so a backtest can see a market that never existed, and nobody can prove what a venue actually showed at 14:37. We build the opposite: every figure keeps its source, its arrival time and its age; gaps stay gaps; venues stay separate; the past stays as it was seen.

Programme No. 1 · our own trading runs on this data

A live algorithmic-trading testbed

The programme trades. We study how.

The programme runs on the same store Studio writes: two instances on live crypto markets, using the company’s and its members’ own capital. Each pair is scored on dozens of signals, the score becomes a decision, and the decision is written down before the order goes out — what it saw, what weighed, what the risk was and what it turned down. Backtest, dry run and live trading execute the same code, so a record can be replayed against the journal rather than taken on trust. This is not a service and not a track record for sale; it is how we find out whether the data we keep is good enough to act on.

  1. 01

    The same code

    Backtest, dry run and live trading on the same code — what trades is the code that was tested.

  2. 02

    Every decision recorded

    Why it entered, which signals weighed, what the risk was and what was rejected — written at decision time, not reconstructed afterwards.

  3. 03

    Two instances — LUKO and BYKO

    Two members, two accounts, two configurations, the same market data. Manual interventions and copied entries are marked separately in the journal.

  4. 04

    Live telemetry

    More than a hundred and fifty markets every 120 seconds; the tables of cycles, decisions and market snapshots are open.

First question: how much of a backtest survives a live market, and why — in progress. So far no strategy has shown an edge after costs.

The code is public (MIT) at github.com/blyn-ai; a packaged release for self-hosting is in preparation.

Liability limitation and company status

This English text is a convenience translation. The Lithuanian version at blynai.eu/lt/ is authoritative.

MB „Blynai“ is not a financial institution, an investment manager or a provider of financial services. The company gives no investment advice, does not accept or hold third parties’ money or crypto-assets, and does not operate third parties’ exchange accounts. The company trades only with its own funds; published results are research data, not an offer or a forecast.

MB „Blynai“ is a two-member research and engineering company. Its activity is the development of algorithmic-trading software, the study of how it runs on the company’s own funds, and the publication of results.

Trading with own funds

The software runs as two independent instances — LUKO and BYKO. The same code, separate accounts, separate journals.

  • trading is done only with the funds of the company and its members;
  • each instance runs in its own exchange account, with its own API keys;
  • the strategy, risk limits and parameters are set by the members themselves;
  • no client money — there is none and there will be none.

No third-party assets

The company:

  • does not accept or hold third parties’ money or crypto-assets;
  • does not operate third parties’ exchange accounts;
  • neither holds nor requests API permissions that would allow it to dispose of another person’s funds;
  • does not carry out collective investment or the management of pooled capital;
  • does not accept third-party funds for trading, collective investment or managed investment activity, and gives no individual investment advice.

Published results

Published data are the historical research data of a specific instance over a specific period. They do not guarantee future results and are not an offer, a forecast or individual investment advice. Failed experiments are published the same way as successful ones.

Open source

The software is distributed under an open-source model so that a result can be reproduced by a third party without the company’s involvement. It is provided as is, without warranties. Anyone deploying it does so on infrastructure they control, in their own exchange account and at their own risk; the company makes no trading decisions on their behalf and does not control their account or funds.
